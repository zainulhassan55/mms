International Journal of Digital Health (IJDH)
Aims & Scope
Health systems are being rebuilt around data, connectivity, and remote care. Electronic health records, telemedicine platforms, wearable sensors, mobile diagnostics, and clinical decision-support models now sit beside bedside practice, promising earlier detection, continuity across settings, and more personalized treatment. Those capabilities remain uneven. Design-time barriers include fragmented data models, weak interoperability across hospitals and devices, scarce labeled clinical datasets, and algorithms that fail to generalize across populations, devices, and care pathways. At runtime, digital health services must stay synchronized with evolving guidelines, integrate with legacy hospital information systems, and fail safely when connectivity, sensors, or models degrade. Privacy law, consent, cybersecurity, and the need for clinical validation further constrain what can be deployed at scale. Progress therefore depends on methods that are clinically meaningful, technically robust, and trustworthy enough for high-stakes patient care.
The International Journal of Digital Health (IJDH) is a peer-reviewed international journal that aims to provide a high-quality platform for the dissemination of innovative research in digital health. The journal focuses on emerging theories, algorithms, systems, and applications that address the evolving challenges of connected care, health data, and intelligent clinical support. IJDH welcomes original clinical and computational studies, system implementations, validation reports, datasets, surveys, and practice-oriented case studies that demonstrate safe, equitable, and interoperable digital health. The journal seeks to promote interdisciplinary collaboration among researchers, engineers, and practitioners in medicine, nursing, public health, biomedical informatics, engineering, and health services research, while fostering the development of interpretable, secure, and clinically useful digital health solutions. By encouraging both theoretical advancements and practical implementations, the journal contributes to shaping the future of connected, patient-centered, and evidence-based care.

Plagiarism
Plagiarism detection is performed at 
the Journal using the Turnitin
 tool. This web-based tool is employed in the editorial process to identify potential text plagiarism. It's important to note that while 
Turnitin
 can identify matching text, it cannot independently determine whether plagiarism has occurred. Manual examination of the matching text is still necessary, and judgment must be exercised to ascertain the presence or absence of plagiarism. The similarity report might be sent to the author for revision whenever needed.
